BAMS Prototype Expansion (BPE) is introduced to amplify scarce human corrections by propagating prototype labels to nearby samples in feature space, thereby enlarging the supervised signal without additional manual annotation. BAMS-selected samples are treated as prototypes because they combine high model uncertainty (margin) with strong local spatial heterogeneity (boundary score), and their Human_Class labels provide trusted anchors at decision-boundary regions where WorldCover errors are concentrated. On Changsha, BPE achieves OA=0.9358, which improves BAMS150 (OA=0.9302, Δ=+0.0056) and does not surpass BAMS150+TeacherStudent (OA=0.9418, Δ=-0.0060). For the minority/target Class 3, BPE reaches Recall=0.9787 and F1=0.9770 (vs BAMS150 Recall=0.9693, F1=0.9723; ΔRecall=+0.0093, ΔF1=+0.0048), indicating that prototype expansion also strengthens Class-3 detection. Overall, cosine-neighborhood label propagation from BAMS prototypes, followed by teacher confidence filtering (conf>0.9 and agreement with the assigned pseudo label) and weighted student training (real=1.0, expanded=0.3), offers a practical route to densify boundary-aware supervision from a compact set of 150 human labels.
